Bureaucracy Battles — Privacy policy

Last updated: 16 October 2026

Bureaucracy Battles is a strategy game for Android by Kim Sneppen, Copenhagen, Denmark (“I”, “me” below). This policy explains what happens to information when you play it.

In short: I collect no personal data. The game has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and I run no servers that could receive anything from it. If you choose to play online, the name you type and your moves go directly to the phones of the people you play with.

What is stored on your phone

The game keeps three small items in its own storage on your device:

  • whether you have unlocked the full office, so it stays unlocked;
  • the name you typed for online play, so you do not have to type it again;
  • the results of your games against the computer (the scores, the computer’s level and the date), for your own statistics on the My scores page.

None of these is sent to me or to anyone else. Uninstalling the game deletes them all.

What I collect

Nothing. The game contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking code, and it does not ask for an account, your contacts, your location or any other personal information.

In-app purchase

The optional purchase of the full office is handled entirely by Google Play Billing. The game only receives a confirmation that the purchase was made and never sees your payment details. Google’s handling of your payment is covered by Google’s privacy policy.

Online play

Online play is optional; nothing below happens unless you open Online and host or join a game. When you do:

  • Other players. The name you typed, your moves and a checksum of the board are sent to the devices of the players in the same game, over an encrypted direct connection (WebRTC).
  • Finding each other. To find each other, the devices post short-lived connection requests to public Nostr relays (currently nos.lol, relay.primal.net, relay.snort.social, nostr-pub.wellorder.net, relay.mostro.network, relay.sigit.io and relay-rpi.edufeed.org). A request contains the technical network details needed to set up the direct connection, such as your device’s IP address, and is encrypted with a key derived from the five-letter office code. Your name and moves are not part of it.
  • Network address. To find its public network address, your device asks STUN servers run by Google and Cloudflare.
  • When a direct connection is blocked. If your networks do not allow a direct connection, the game may pass the encrypted game traffic through a relay (TURN) server of the Open Relay Project (metered.ca).

Like any internet server, these relays and servers see your device’s IP address. They are run by independent third parties under their own policies. I do not operate them and receive nothing from them. Nothing about your games is stored by me.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Your choices

  • To keep everything on your phone, simply do not use online play; the rest of the game works offline.
  • To delete your game results, use Clear my scores on the My scores page.
  • To remove everything the game stores, uninstall it. A purchase can be restored afterwards through Google Play.
