▸ Merging & Creation networks

This applet illustrates a dynamic network which naturally evolves to a steady state where nodes of a large variety of sizes appear. In fact, for some k values, and more robustly for the directed network, the resulting network becomes scale free. There exists little evidence for such dynamics actually playing a role in real-world networks, but for the directed network one may imagine the merging events as company mergers, and the links as business connections. The undirected networks, on the other hand, may be seen as simplified models of the merging of magnetic field lines in the solar atmosphere, as envisioned by Hughes et al. (2003), ref. 4. The variations with p away from 0.5 are taken from a preprint by Riz Noronha and Kim Sneppen (ref. 5).

About the models

Undirected merging & creation. At every step two nodes are merged into one (a node and one of its neighbors, or two random “foreign” nodes) and the merged node inherits the union of their links. Then one new node is created with k links to random nodes (or, in the weighted variant, to nodes chosen with probability ∝ degree). Merging makes hubs grow, creation feeds small nodes in; the steady state is a scale-free network with P(k) ∝ k−γ (Kim, Trusina, Minnhagen & Sneppen 2005).

Directed donors & acceptors. Every node is either a donor (+, blue), with only outgoing arrows, or an acceptor (−, red), with only incoming arrows. An arrow of thickness w = 1, 2, 3, … stands for w individual arrows between the same pair. At every step a random node A is merged with a partner B: with probability p (slider in the upper-right corner of the display) B is a random node of the same sign, otherwise a random node of the opposite sign. p = 0.5 corresponds to picking B uniformly among all nodes; p = 1 only pools, p = 0 only rewires. If A and B have the same sign, A+B takes over all their arrows, so arrows to a common neighbor add up and get thicker. If they have opposite signs, the merged node takes the sign of the one with more arrow units (ties at random), and every arrow end of the other sign is rewired, one unit at a time: a random incoming unit C→A is paired with a random outgoing unit B→D and replaced by a new arrow C→D between the former neighbors. Any arrows between A and B themselves are removed. The result is a pure node A+B with |kA−kB| units and new arrows spread among the old neighbors, which lets thick arrows dissolve into thinner ones. Whenever the number of nodes drops to N−2, two nodes are created: a donor with k outgoing units to random acceptors and an acceptor with k incoming units from random donors. In the second variant the opposite-sign partner is not a random node but a neighbor reached along one of A's arrows (chosen ∝ thickness); same-sign partners are still random nodes.

Parameters. N is the number of nodes at seeding, k the number of links given to each new node (fractional k is rounded stochastically). Speed is the number of merge steps per second. Node volume is ∝ degree (or √degree). Layout: nodes repel as k1k2/d3, links act as springs, and neighbors of the same hub repel each other (angle widening).

Plots. Left: current degree distribution (circles, blue donors / red acceptors in the directed model, yellow diamonds for the distribution of arrow thickness w), the time-averaged P(k) over the last 3/4 of the run (orange) and a power-law fit for 5 ≤ k < kmax/10. Middle: distribution of merge event sizes s, the number of link ends that disappear when two nodes merge (2 per lost link; in the directed model a thick arrow counts as w unit arrows), accumulated over all merges since the last reseed, with a power-law fit for 10 ≤ s < smax/10. Fits never use points below k = 5 or s = 10. Right: the three largest degrees versus time (click the button on the plot to switch the window).

Display. Each merge sets off a flash of D small stars around the merged node, where D is the number of link ends that dissolved; the stars fill a sphere whose volume is proportional to D, fly outward and fade over the “Flash time” set in the panel (yellow on a white background, white on black). Pause also freezes the rotation. In the directed model the arrows are shaded cylinders running from the donor surface to a cone whose tip touches the acceptor surface; their width grows as √w or ∜w (button under “Link width”), capped at the diameter of the thinner end node. The two buttons in the lower-left corner of the display toggle a black/white background and save a square .png that just contains the whole network.
