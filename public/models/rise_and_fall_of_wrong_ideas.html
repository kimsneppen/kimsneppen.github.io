Rise and Fall of Wrong Ideas what is this?

Here is a curious thing about ideas. A really new one is hard to come by — you may wait a long time for that spark, but once it begins to be accepted it travels easily, from one person to the next, until nearly everybody has it. And then it wears out. Not all at once, but slowly, by being chipped at by new ideas about things entirely different. And here is the point of this model dealing explicitly with wrong ideas: they do not come back. You have heard that argument once; nobody is going to talk you into it a second time.

That is really the whole model, and it is simple enough that you can watch it work. Picture a crowd of little people, each holding one idea, drawn as one colour. Every so often one of them turns to a neighbour and says, "listen to this." Now the neighbour is a sensible fellow. He does not just take the idea; he asks a third person, somebody within his horizon, "have you heard of this?" — and he adopts it only if that third person already holds it. So a popular idea gets confirmed all the time and spreads like fire, while a brand-new idea has almost nobody to vouch for it and usually dies at birth. Only once in a while — at the innovation rate α — somebody invents a thing nobody has ever held, and once in a while, by luck, it catches on.

And then the one rule that makes it interesting: a person never returns to an idea he has once given up (or not until a long memory time τ has passed). Think about what that does. An old idea cannot defend itself against a new one, because every bit of ground it has lost is lost for good. So a paradigm rises fast, rules for a while, gets eaten away from the edges, and collapses — to be replaced by the next one, which will suffer the same fate. Look at the curves under the display: steep rise, slow decline, over and over. That asymmetry is the whole story, and it comes out of nothing more than the difficulty of inventing something truly new compared with the ease of eroding it.

Physicists have a name for what is going on: the local rule breaks detailed balance. Nothing ever flows back the way it came, so old states cannot hold their own against new states nucleating or invading — a pattern-formation game quite unlike the familiar ones, where the strongest state simply wins and stays. Sometimes several ideas freeze side by side for a long while, coexisting as metastable states, until an invention breaks the stalemate. Try it: run on the lattice, then on a social network, then let the network itself rewire around the ideas, and see what changes and what does not.

Model & controls

What you see

Every agent holds exactly one idea, and each idea has its own colour. On the 2-d lattice and the Sierpiński carpet an agent is one coloured cell; on the network it is a little person (or a sphere) sitting on a node, joined to its acquaintances by links. Under the display, the first pull-down plots the population of the five most widespread ideas against time together with the diameter of the geometry, and the second shows the connectivity distribution P(k).

The rules

A random agent tries to pass its idea along one of its connections (a lattice bond, or a network link). The receiving agent i accepts only if a third agent, drawn at random within the Horizon of i, already holds that same idea — on the lattice the horizon is a Euclidean distance, on the network a shortest-path distance in hops. When the horizon covers the whole system this is exactly the cooperative factor nj/N of the paper. Crucially, i accepts only if it has never held that idea before (or abandoned it more than τ sweeps ago). With probability α per update a random agent instead invents an entirely new idea. Time is counted in sweeps: one sweep is one update per agent.

Buttons on top of the display

They mirror the main controls of this panel, so everything can be driven from the display alone — handy on a phone.

▶ / ❚❚ runs or pauses the simulation. Step advances one display step of Δt sweeps and redraws. Reset keeps the geometry but returns every agent to one common idea, so a run starts from a single paradigm.

Sierp, 2-d and Net choose the geometry: the Sierpiński carpet (agents only on the 8k filled cells, holes are dark), the periodic square lattice with N = L² agents, or a three-dimensional social network (modular, Erdős–Rényi or power-law; presets A, B, C in the Network pull-down).

S.org switches on the self-organised network: links follow the ideas. When a spread i → k is confirmed by agent r, the bond k–r is created and paid for by deleting one link on a random shortest path i → r, so the number of links is conserved while the network reshapes itself around the ideas.

G1 / G2 start a game on the current geometry. A player's idea is true: unlike every other idea it may be taken up again by an agent who once abandoned it, so the converted can be re-enlightened. Agents holding a true idea are drawn 1.6× larger. In each round a player plants the idea on the chosen number of agents (slider nodes) by clicking them, the system then runs, and play returns when the idea has died out. Game 1 (one player) is won when the true idea holds 10 % of the agents at once; in Game 2 (two players, red and blue) the winner is whoever first reaches 10 % — at one moment, or counted over all agents ever convinced.

◐ toggles a black or white background in every panel. ☻ cycles how network nodes are drawn: little people → people 50 % larger → plain spheres. A person's height grows with the number of acquaintances, the right arm points at whoever convinced him, and a golden halo marks the head where an idea was born. sq in the lower-left corner saves a square PNG of exactly what is on the display.

Main parameters

Innovation rate α — probability per elementary update that a random agent invents a new idea; the hint shows αN, the number of inventions per sweep. Memory τ — how long an abandoned idea stays forbidden; τ = ∞ is the base model. Horizon — how far away the confirming third agent may live (default 3 hops on the network, 2 cells on the lattice; slide to the top for the whole system, as in the paper). Δt — sweeps between two frames, tdisplay — width of the plot window.

Interactions

Drag = orbit · scroll = zoom · click a node or cell = plant a brand-new idea there (during a game: plant the player's idea).
