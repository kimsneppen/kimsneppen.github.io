Interface propagation in quenched noise

min r on the front → h+1 → relax until |∇h| ≤ 1

Interface dynamics as a model system for more (*)

Interfaces. Stochastic interfaces are studied as prototypes of systems with many interconnected degrees of freedom. The classical models are the Edwards–Wilkinson equation [1] and the KPZ equation [2], which generalize the random walk to long random strings and surfaces — roughening without, respectively with, a drift generated by the growth itself. Later models coupled the dynamics over long distances, most elegantly by allowing only the site with the minimal quenched random number to advance: with that one rule the front organizes itself to a critical state, moving in avalanches of all sizes. These extremal models [3–5] are the prime focus of this applet, with the randomly driven counterparts (KPZ, Lap) included for comparison.

A front h(x) creeps through a medium in which every lattice point (x, h) carries a frozen random resistance r ∈ [0,1], drawn once and never redrawn (quenched noise). The dynamics is extremal: only the single weakest point on the whole front moves [3].

1. find (x,y) with the smallest r on the front  →  2. h → h + 1 there  →  3. relax until |∇h| ≤ 1 everywhere

The relaxation is the lateral growth rule: whenever a neighbour lags more than one unit behind, it is pulled up by one — and this is propagated outward through the lattice until every nearest-neighbour slope satisfies |Δh| ≤ 1. Every newly reached site gets a fresh random number, so the noise never has to be stored ahead of the front and the interface can advance forever.

The model is self-organized critical: without tuning any parameter, the front settles into the state where the smallest resistances it can find sit right at a threshold rc. In 1+1 dimensions rc ≈ 0.461 — one minus the site directed-percolation threshold — and the pinning paths are directed-percolation clusters of strong sites; this relation between the Sneppen model and directed percolation is analysed in detail by Olami, Procaccia & Zeitak [8]. Closely related interface models were built directly on directed percolation — the porous-media model of Buldyrev et al. [6], introduced together with imbibition experiments in paper, and the pinning model of Tang & Leschhorn [7] — but there the driving force is fine-tuned to the critical point of directed percolation, whereas the extremal dynamics organizes itself to that point without any tuning. Growth then proceeds in bursts of all sizes, and the interface roughens as W ∝ Lχ with χ = 0.63 in 1+1 D and χ = 0.50 ± 0.03 in 2+1 D (Kardar–Parisi–Zhang would give 0.40, Edwards–Wilkinson only log L).

Time is counted in units of added height per site, i.e. t = ⟨h⟩.

About the model & parameters

The rule. Every point (x, y, h) of the medium holds a frozen random resistance r ∈ [0,1]. At each update the globally smallest r on the front is located, that column advances by one, h → h+1, and it meets a brand-new random number. Then neighbours are pulled up, one unit at a time and propagated outward, until every nearest-neighbour slope obeys |Δh| ≤ 1. Since r is only needed where the front actually is, nothing has to be stored ahead of it and the front may advance forever.

Why it is critical. Advancing the weakest site keeps opening fresh territory next to the last advance, so activity clusters into bursts. Small resistances get used up, and the front self-organizes to the state where the smallest available r hovers at the pinning threshold rc (≈ 0.461 in 1+1 D). The read-out rc is the largest selected minimum so far: it climbs towards that threshold and, at finite L, overshoots it by a few percent. In 2+1 D the same read-out settles much lower — the four-neighbour relaxation keeps opening fresh sites, so weak spots are never in short supply.

Parameters.

  • rule — Sneppen: relax until every nearest-neighbour slope obeys |Δh| ≤ 1. L.-H. Tang: relax on the curvature instead — relax until |∇²h| ≤ 2 everywhere, with ∇²h = ∂²h/∂x² + ∂²h/∂y². A site that lags behind is pulled up itself; a site that juts out is repaired by pulling up its lowest neighbour, with ties broken at random — repair it at an arbitrary neighbour and the two corrections chase each other for ever, while resolving ties by a fixed direction draws straight ridges across the front. The relaxation runs in shells: the four neighbours of the advanced site first, then everything neighbouring whatever moved, and so on, each site taking at most one step per shell. Neighbours are visited from a random direction, clockwise or anticlockwise, chosen afresh at each advance: the order decides which sites meet which fresh random numbers. Bounding the curvature rather than the slope lets the front tilt freely, so it roughens far more than the Sneppen front.
  • dimension — 1D: a line front h(x) with noise r(x,h) and two neighbours; 2D: a surface h(x,y) on an L×L base with r(x,y,h) and four neighbours.
  • L — linear system size. The roughness saturates at W ∝ Lχ, χ ≈ 0.63 (1D) and 0.50 (2D), after a time ∝ Lχ.
  • boundaries — periodic (as in the papers) or open, where the end columns simply have one neighbour fewer and the front is free to tilt.
  • steps / frame — extremal updates per drawn frame, from 1 up to L (1D) or L2 (2D) in steps of √2. One sweep of Ld updates advances the front by roughly one layer, Δt ≈ 1.
  • colour by — r on the front (the pinning field the interface currently sits in; the pinned regions light up above rc), h−⟨h⟩ (the shape itself), or |∇h| measured over the window x−2 … x+2, which highlights the steep facets left by bursts.
  • Transp / view — in the 2+1 D surface, Transp pauses the run, takes one more step and drapes everything swallowed in that step over the old front as a translucent grey layer. Click that layer and it separates: the front disappears and the swallowed volume is left alone, turning slowly, draggable. Switch Transp off to go back and carry on. The view button reaches the same solid directly, in either dimension. The last ten interfaces are kept, and ◀ walks the pair on show back through them one step at a time — in the surface and in the isolated solid alike — wrapping round to the newest.
  • display — 1D: single profile, stacked profiles, the h(x,t) surface in perspective, or a space–time image; 2D: colour map of the base plane, or the front as a shaded 3D surface.

Keys: space run/pause, s single frame, r reset, +/− background.
